feat: full AI curriculum across every major branch

Catalog goes from 6 courses to 27 (178 modules), 22 of which are AI. The AI
material was previously one flat "AI & ML" bucket; it is now split into nine
branches so a student can actually see the shape of the field.

  AI Foundations   (4)  what AI is, search, logic & knowledge representation,
                        expert systems
  Machine Learning (4)  foundations, supervised, unsupervised, reinforcement
  Deep Learning    (3)  networks, architectures, training & optimisation
  Generative AI    (3)  transformers/LLMs, generative models, prompting & RAG
  Natural Language (1)  preprocessing through QA and translation
  Computer Vision  (1)  classification, detection, segmentation, multimodal
  Speech & Audio   (1)  ASR, TTS, speaker recognition, music
  Robotics         (1)  perception, planning, control, SLAM, self-driving
  Responsible AI   (4)  ethics & fairness, safety & alignment, production,
                        applications by industry

Classical AI is deliberately included alongside the modern material. Search,
logic, expert systems and knowledge graphs are real branches that a
"machine-learning-only" catalog quietly drops, and they are also the parts
students can reason about most easily.

Structural changes
- The catalog moved out of the route file into src/data/curriculum.ts. It is
  content, not request handling, and it had outgrown the file it lived in.
- Boot now syncs category/description/level, not just modules. Previously an
  edited category or an extended syllabus only reached brand-new databases;
  existing installs silently kept serving the old version. Since modules are
  the denominator for a student's progress percentage, a stale syllabus
  quietly corrupts numbers rather than just looking out of date.

Retiring courses
Renaming "AI in Practice" to "AI in Production" left the old course in the
database forever, because the seeder only ever inserted by title. Retired
titles are now an explicit allowlist, and a retired course is never deleted
while a student is still enrolled in it - the enrolment stores the courseId
inline, so deleting it would orphan real progress. Losing history is a better
failure than an enrolment pointing at nothing.

Verified: 83/83 UI checks (4 new, pinning the branch set, course count, a
minimum syllabus depth, and total module count), 13/13 security, both TS
projects compile.
