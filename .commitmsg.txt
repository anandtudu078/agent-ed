fix: grade students in their own language; rate limit per student

Two defects that would have hurt real classrooms, both invisible in testing.

1. Assessments ignored the student's language
   The question was always generated in English and graded in English. A Hindi
   student could explain backpropagation correctly in Hindi and be marked down
   for their English - graded on the wrong subject entirely, which made the
   bilingual feature actively harmful for the students it was built for.

   The question is now asked in the student's language, and the grading prompt
   says explicitly that an answer in Hindi must never be scored lower. That
   instruction is unconditional and specific because it is exactly the kind of
   thing a grader quietly violates when left to its own judgement - the old
   prompt already said "don't judge polish or grammar" and it wasn't enough.
   Language is read from the JWT, never the request body, so a client cannot
   request a Hindi question and have it graded against English criteria.

   Verified live: a correct Hinglish answer was graded on its substance
   (spotting the genuinely missing chain-rule detail) and the feedback came
   back in Hindi, with the score driven by the content and not the script.

2. Rate limits were per-IP, which breaks a shared network
   Schools put a whole classroom behind one public IP, so 12 chats/minute per
   IP meant students throttling each other mid-lesson while the error blamed
   whoever sent last. The AI budget is now keyed on the signed-in user, which
   is the honest unit: one student's usage is one student's usage.

   Auth limits get the same treatment for a different reason: 30 students
   signing in one morning exhausted a shared 10-per-15-minutes budget. They are
   now keyed on IP+username with successful logins not counted. Per-username
   alone would hand a brute-forcer a fresh budget per guess, and per-IP alone
   locks out a classroom, so it is both - with the trade-off documented at the
   definition rather than left for someone to rediscover.

   Verified live: one student exhausted their budget at exactly 12 requests
   (then 429) while a second student on the same IP got clean 200s.

83/83 UI checks, 13/13 security, both TS projects compile.
